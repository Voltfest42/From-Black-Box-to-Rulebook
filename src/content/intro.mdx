In the mid-1990s, a team of researchers trained models to predict which pneumonia patients were likely to die, so that the low-risk ones could safely be treated at home. The most accurate models were neural networks. They were never used on patients.

The reason was a rule. A simpler, rule-based model trained on the same data had learned: **IF the patient has asthma THEN their risk is lower.** The data really did say that. Asthmatics who came in with pneumonia were usually sent straight to intensive care, and the extra care worked. A model that sent them home because of it would have put them in danger.

If the rules had learned that, the networks surely had too. But nobody could see it inside a network, or know what else was hidden in there.

This site is about getting rules like that one *out* of machine-learning models: how it's done, and how the techniques have evolved from 1988 to 2026, one live demo at a time.
